# 0012: Retheme v2 as a 2050 "holographic glass" site (impact assessment)

## Status
Accepted (2026-11-11), against the approved `docs/design-spec-2050.md` (commit f23777f) and `docs/requirements/v2-theme-2050.md`. It was Proposed earlier the same day as an impact assessment; the sections below are kept as written, and "Accepted decisions" records what was settled.

This ADR:
- **for v2 only**, replaces v1 FR-2 (greyscale), FR-3 (CRT look), FR-4 (teletext typeface), FR-5 (line, dot, static, test card warm-up) and FR-6 (test card), and the greyscale guarantee of architecture §4.5;
- amends ADR 0005 (one rasteriser, two outputs: kept; the CRT material becomes a holo material);
- keeps ADR 0003 and ADR 0004 (facts) and ADR 0011 (environment player) unchanged.

**v1 on `main` stays the 1970s release, and none of this applies to it.**

### 4. Bundle and performance

**Frosted glass (`backdrop-filter`) over a WebGL canvas.**

*Why it is expensive:* every frame the canvas changes, the compositor re-reads and blurs the area behind each frosted element. Cost grows with area times blur radius, nested frosted layers multiply it, and it is worst on mobile and in Firefox. Over a 30 to 60 Hz canvas this can cost more than the scene itself.

*Mitigations, all required:*
1. Live blur only on small chrome (dock, info row, HUD tags), at `blur()` of 12 px or less, with no nested frosted layers.
2. Large surfaces (guide panel, dialogs, side panel) use tinted glass with **no** live blur (a semi-opaque gradient plus a noise texture), or a blur that applies only while the canvas is paused (Calm, `demand` idle).
3. A `data-glass="solid"` switch on `<html>`, set when `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` applies, when `backdrop-filter` is unsupported, or when `AdaptiveDpr` has stepped down. This gives opaque panels, and it is also the low-end path.
4. **Text contrast must never depend on the scene:** every glass panel has a minimum tint so text meets 4.5:1 against both the darkest and the brightest backdrop (QA section 3).
5. The canvas runs ambient motion at 30 Hz (the performance pass), so live blur re-runs at 30 Hz, not 60.

*The YouTube rule is unchanged and extended:*
- No ancestor of the player may have `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `opacity` below 1, `mix-blend-mode`, `mask`, `clip-path` or a 3D transform.
- **No frosted or glowing element may overlap the player rect.** This includes `box-shadow`, `outline` and glow halos: keep the 24 px halo rule of design spec v2 for DOM chrome too.
- A frosted panel over the player would filter YouTube's pixels, which is an overlay and a modification. ADR 0011 §1's guarantee list and the QA ancestor and hit tests gain `backdrop-filter`.
- `classifyPlacement` is unaffected. The frontend's layout must keep panels outside the rect.

**Volumetric light** (beams from the emitter, holo glow, room haze):
- No ray-marched volumetrics and no full-screen post-processing on phones.
- Use additive, camera-facing cone or shell meshes with soft gradient textures, sorted, with depth write off. That is 1 or 2 draw calls and a bounded overdraw.
- Glow comes from the holo material's own LOD sample, as the CRT glow does now, plus additive sprite halos. No `EffectComposer` bloom.
- Desktop selective bloom is allowed only with a new ADR (it adds `postprocessing`) and measured figures. It would never touch the player, because the player is DOM above the canvas.
- Budget for haze, beams and glow together: **2 ms GPU** or less on a 2022 mid-range phone at the adapted DPR, and 1.5 MB or less of extra assets for the TV room (it still sits under v1's TV-room budget).
- Colour does not change draw calls. Additive transparency does raise overdraw, so particle counts go into the budget table: 2,000 or fewer on mobile, 10,000 or fewer on desktop, instanced.

**Bundle:**
- New fonts are likely 20 to 60 kB of WOFF2; VT323 (16 kB) and Barlow (44.5 kB) leave v2.
- The holo shader and `HoloDisplay` are code, about the size of what they replace.
- No new runtime package in the plan. The first-view budget (NFR-3) and the 10 kB v2 initial-JS allowance are unchanged.

## Consequences
- **Easier:** the expensive parts survive the retheme: the truthful-intro pipeline, the story engine (clock, scheduler, cues, effects), the frame and pagination model, the YouTube player and guide, and all nine room contracts. Most work is new art, a shader rewrite behind an existing seam, and CSS.
- **Harder:**
  - Colour removes the cheapest guarantee v1 had (R = G = B by construction) and adds the red-flash rule.
  - Glass makes text contrast depend on what is behind it, unless panels have a minimum tint.
  - Live blur over WebGL is the main new performance risk; it is contained by blurring only small chrome and by the solid fallback.
- **Branch divergence:** v1 and v2 diverge permanently in look. Fixes to shared engines (facts, clock, scheduler) must be applied to both branches by hand.

## Questions only the user can answer
None remain. The two design choices this section raised were settled by the user on 2026-11-11 (see "Accepted decisions"): Space Mono keeps the rasteriser core, and the Colour switch stays, with Colour on by default.
